How the NFL numbers are made

Status. The NFL board is in tracking mode. The NBA model showed a small edge in testing; the NFL one has not. Nothing here is a recommended stake: picks are logged at a paper 1u and graded so the model can be judged on real prices.

Projection. Each player's average for the stat from his games with his current team only (recent games count more: half-life 5 games; this season counts fully, last season 35%), nudged by how much his team is expected to score this week versus its average. The nudge was tuned on five seasons of prop lines built from prior games and turned out small (zero for most stats).

Chance of clearing the line. The projection is compared with how far real outcomes have landed from projections of that size, by stat and band, to get the model's chance of the over. The model's average is then centred on the bookies' average for that stat (the model runs low on volume stats), so a pick needs a player whose gap differs from his stat's typical gap.

Blend. The bookies' no-vig chance (average across Sportsbet and PointsBet at the same line) is blended with the model in log-odds: 0.954 market + 0.343 model. Those weights were fitted on NBA props and are untested on the NFL. A pick's value is the blended chance times the best price on that side, minus 1; 5% or more is a candidate.

Flags. Each candidate is checked against the injury report (Out, Doubtful, Questionable, limited or no practice), snap counts (a share that moved 20+ points in the last two games), teammates at the same position who are out, history with the current team (under 3 games), and a model/market gap of 25+ points. Any flag turns Tracked into Check news, which is never a pick. Most big model-versus-market gaps turn out to be role information the bookie has and the model doesn't.

Bookmakers. Sportsbet and PointsBet, read from their public pages without logging in. Prop lines can differ by a half step between them; each is priced on its own line. Ladbrokes and Betr are not yet included. Props appear a few days before kick-off, so the board fills in over the week; Sportsbet had props for 5 of 14 games on Friday, PointsBet for all 14.

First touchdown scorer. Priced from the bookie's own markets, not a trained model: the first-scorer price (margin removed) averaged with each player's share of the game's scoring implied by his anytime-TD price. The margin on this market is large, so bets are rare, and they are not graded yet.

Team model and game bets. A ratings model for games was backtested against closing lines over five seasons: it adds nothing, and any edge is in early-week prices that shrink as lines move toward it. Sportsbet's NFL same-game multis already charge for the links between legs. So there are no game, multi or same-game bets here.

Grading. After each round the logged picks (and every prop priced, not just the picks) are graded against official player stats: log loss for the market, the model and the blend, and the best blend weight on the pooled results. This needs about six weeks of logged prices before the weights mean anything; until then the ledger is a plumbing check, not evidence.

Alternate lines

"To score 30+" style ladders from Sportsbet, Ladbrokes and PointsBet. First basket

The model's chance that each starter scores the game's first points, against every bookie's First Basket price. Two things drive it. The tip-off: the team that wins the jump ball scores first 66% of the time, and centres' tip records are tracked jump by jump. Who shoots first: each starter's share of his team's first baskets, built from his scoring (flattened, because first baskets are spread more evenly than points) with a lift for centres and the jumper, and nudged by his own first-basket record. Bench players scored first in 0 of 3,744 games. Tested on 1,278 games (2025-26 and 2026-27, using only earlier games each time): it beat "every starter equally likely" and was well calibrated, but it has never been tested against past first-basket odds, so it only flags a bet at 10%+ value, at a flat 0.25u, and every bet is tracked in the ledger. The chance counts free throws as a first basket (8.5% of first scores are free throws); check your bookie's rule. Bookie margins on this market are about 15%.

Same-game multis

Two-leg same-game multis priced by PointsBet (the only bookie that quotes them without logging in), against our chance of both legs landing. Legs in one game move together, so the chance isn't the two chances multiplied: we link them with correlations measured on 1,275 games of 2024-25 (team result and game total against each player's stats, and a player's stats against each other). One finding shapes everything: PointsBet's SGM prices already allow for these links closely, including the blowout effect (a big favourite's starters sit, so their stats barely rise with the margin), and they carry a margin of roughly 10-25%. So we only pair legs our model already rates above PointsBet's single price; the value is those leg edges compounding. A bet needs 10%+ value and a 15%+ chance of landing, at a flat 0.25u, one per game, and every one is tracked in the ledger.

Player combos

Same-game multis on one player: two or three stat milestones together, like 15+ points, 4+ rebounds and 4+ assists. A player's stats rise and fall together (more minutes, a good night), so these land more often than the legs multiplied: on real 2024-25 prop lines, two of a player's stats landed 7.5% more often than the market's chances multiplied, and points + rebounds + assists triples 25% more. Sportsbet prices these at about the legs multiplied (triples slightly above), so the link is often not paid for there; PointsBet cuts them by about 5%. Our chance starts from each bookie's own single prices with its margin taken off (the market beats a player's game log on single stats), then adds the link for that stat pair. A bet needs 8%+ value and a 25%+ chance, flat 0.25u, one per player, tracked in the ledger as "Combo".

How the numbers are made

Player ratings. Basketball-Reference per-100 Off/Def rating and Box Plus/Minus (BPM). This season's 2–4 games are blended with last season's (or 2024-25 for players who missed it), weighted by minutes.

Team ratings. Expected minutes × player BPM sets each team's net rating for tonight; missing minutes go to replacement level (−2 BPM). That prior is blended with the team's actual results so far.

Game projection. Pace × offence vs opposing defence, plus 1.9 points home court, then adjusted for schedule: back-to-back (−2.5), rest advantage (+0.45 a day, up to 2) and distance flown (−0.3 per 1,000 miles, up to −1). A team on a back-to-back also takes 1 point off the total.

Market anchor. The final number is 70% the bookie consensus and 30% model. The consensus averages every bookie's moneyline, half-point line and total with the margin removed (Sportsbet, TAB, Ladbrokes, PointsBet, Betr); when only one bookie prices the game it is 65% that bookie and 35% model. Only firm disagreements become bets, and a bet only exists if the bookie's price beats that fair price.

Probabilities. Final margins are whole numbers with no draws, so margin chances come from a normal curve (SD 13.7) over whole numbers with zero left out; totals use a normal curve (SD 17.9). Bookie prices have their margin removed, and when lines differ the fair price is moved to each bookie's line on the same curve.

Props sanity checks. Minutes freed by injuries that no regular can absorb go to the deep bench, and team scoring is never scaled more than 25% either way. A projection more than 30% from the line is marked Check news, not Bet.

Bookmakers. Prices come from Sportsbet, TAB, Ladbrokes (Neds runs on the same platform and usually matches it), PointsBet and Betr, read from their public pages without logging in. Each bookie's main line is compared and the best price wins. A multi uses legs that are all priced at one bookie, because a multi can't be split across bookmakers. bet365 blocks automated reading, so it isn't included.

Backtest. The game model was replayed on 3,744 games (2023-24 to now) using only results known before each game, then compared with DraftKings / ESPN BET / Caesars lines. Settings were tuned on 2024-25 and checked untouched on 2025-26. The model's win chances are well calibrated, but it is less accurate than the closing line and adds nothing to it; betting it at closing prices lost 8–10%. Against the opening line it does carry information: lines move toward the model (strongly significant), and betting openers it disagreed with by 2.5+ points won 55% (2024-25) and 52% (2025-26). So the edge is small and only exists when betting early, before the market moves. The backtest also set home court (1.9), the spreads (13.7 / 17.9) and the schedule adjustments; 3-in-4 nights and altitude did not hold up and were removed.

Removing the bookie's margin. Every chance starts from bookie odds with the margin taken out. For games this uses the power method, which tested best on 3,736 past games: the usual "scale both sides to 100%" method under-rates favourites (bookies put more of their margin on the longshot), e.g. a 1.14 / 6.00 moneyline is 86% for the favourite, not 84%. Player props sit near 50/50, where every method gives the same answer. One-sided markets (alternate lines, "30+ points") can't be de-margined directly, so they are priced from the de-margined main line.

Props backtest. The props model was replayed on 52,000 player lines (ESPN BET 2024-25, DraftKings 2026-27) using only box scores from earlier games. On its own it is less accurate than the bookie's price (log loss 0.699 vs 0.689) and betting it at closing prices lost about 4% (−189u over 4,400 bets in Feb–Apr 2025). It does know something the opening price doesn't: when a line moved, it moved the model's way 79–86% of the time. But betting openers was only break-even (−0.5% to +3% depending on the season and edge size). So the live model now leans on the market (log-odds weights 0.95 market / 0.34 model, wider projection spread), which means far fewer prop bets: most surviving edges come from one bookie being out of line with the others.

Grading the model. Every pick is compared with the last fair price before tip-off (CLV), and win rates are compared with the model's own probabilities by category, so miscalibrated markets show up early.
